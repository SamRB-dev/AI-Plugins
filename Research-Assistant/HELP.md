# Research Assistant: quick help

Type `HELP` for the complete guide, or one of these:

| Command | What it explains |
|---|---|
| `HELP MODES` | REVIEW, CHECK, PLAN, FIND, CHALLENGE, EXPLAIN and WRITE |
| `HELP SOURCES` | Uploading papers, source IDs, registry and verification |
| `HELP PERMISSIONS` | The Level 0-3 authorship permission ladder |
| `HELP WORKFLOW` | Recommended end-to-end research workflow |
| `HELP LOG` | Maintaining the issue log across draft versions |
| `HELP TIPS` | How to get precise, verifiable results |

## Copyable prompts

- `REVIEW: Assess this section. Identify the three most serious weaknesses first. Do not rewrite it.`
- `CHECK: Verify each factual claim against S1-S3. Quote the evidence and give exact locators.`
- `PLAN: Evaluate what to include, shorten, move or cut against my research question and word limit. I make the final decision.`
- `FIND: Locate the exact sampling frequency reported in S2. Quote the passage and give the full locator. If absent, say so.`
- `CHALLENGE: Act as a sceptical examiner. Rank the five strongest objections to my method.`
- `EXPLAIN: Explain this concept in plain language, then give an example and the technical explanation.`
- `WRITE: Draft a 120-word transition paragraph. This is explicit Level 3 permission for this passage only. Use only supplied facts and citations, and list assumptions.`

## Permission levels

- **Level 0 (default):** Diagnose and advise. Do not rewrite.
- **Level 1:** Grammar, spelling and punctuation only. Show every change.
- **Level 2:** One alternative for the named sentence or paragraph, labelled `AI suggestion`. Explain changes. No new claims or citations.
- **Level 3:** Draft new text within the requested scope and length, labelled `AI-written`. List assumptions.

Permission is limited to the passage and request named. Say `back to review only` or `stop writing` to return to Level 0. Source-integrity rules can never be disabled.

## Source verification checklist

1. Upload the actual source documents.
2. Register them as S1, S2, etc. Do not infer metadata from filenames.
3. For each source-based claim, quote the passage first.
4. Give source ID, section, printed page, paragraph, sentence or printed line, and a 5-15-word verbatim anchor.
5. Mark unknown locator fields as `unknown`.
6. Distinguish what is Stated, Implied and Inferred.
7. Never invent references, quotes, page numbers, DOIs, figures or statistics.
